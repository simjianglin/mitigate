The Earth is getting hotter. Let’s find out why.

When we burn coal, oil and gas, we put gases into the air that trap heat. That is climate change.

“Mitigate” means to make something less bad. Here you can learn what causes climate change, what it does, and what small things you can do to help.

Start learning

The coloured stripes are decorative, inspired by climate “warming stripes”. They are not real data.

mit·i·gate verb, /ˈmɪtɪɡeɪt/

To make something less bad. For climate, it means cutting the gases that heat up the Earth.

Its partner word is “adapt”: getting ready for changes that are already coming.

Quick fact

Climate change can feel far too big for one person. But it’s made of everyday choices, and so is the way out.

The goal in three steps

  1. Be ready. Help people stay safe in floods, heatwaves and storms.
  2. Make a plan. Governments and schools set goals to cut pollution.
  3. Learn and share. When more people understand it, more people act.

What can we do?

You don’t have to do everything. Pick what fits your life and tap “I’ll try this” on the ones you mean.

Eat more plants

Plant-rich meals usually have a smaller carbon footprint, and they can be delicious.

Move differently

Walk, cycle or ride public transport for everyday trips, and enjoy the fresh air.

Plant and protect

Planting trees with neighbours cools streets and stores carbon.

Repair and share

Repair cafés and swap events keep things in use and bring people together.

Clean power

Ask leaders to grow solar and wind power and to phase out coal.

Protect wild places

Forests and wetlands soak up carbon and shelter wildlife. They need protecting.
